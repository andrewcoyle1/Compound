# Citation verification checklist

For: `docs/research/algorithms-evidence.md` (reference numbers R1–R113 match that report's **References** list).

**How this was built.** Every factual or numeric claim in the report now carries an [R*n*] citation, a [derived: …] label or a [design choice — no source] label. Each DOI below was then searched on the web (direct Crossref/PubMed/NAP access was blocked by the network proxy). **Status** means:

- **DOI confirmed by search**: a search result (publisher page, PubMed/PMC record, repository record or a citing reference list) showed this DOI attached to this title, authors, year and journal.
- **DOI recalled, unconfirmed**: the DOI comes from the researchers' memory or a pattern match and was not seen in a search result. Click it before relying on it.
- **DOI not found**: locate it by hand from the full reference given.
- **No DOI (URL/book)**: guideline, web page or book; check the URL or the edition.

Confirming a DOI does **not** mean the numbers were confirmed. The "What to check" column says what still needs reading in the source. Tick each row when done.

## Possible discrepancies

Numbers in the report were **not changed**; each item below needs a decision after reading the source.

1. **DRI 2023, men "very active" equation (R14).** One search summary quoting Table S-1 gave men ≥19 y very active as **TEE = −671.75 + 3.68·age + 15.38·H + 23.25·W (R² 0.92, RMSE 259)**; another search and the USP copy gave the report's **−517.88 − 10.83·age + 15.61·H + 19.11·W**. The positive age coefficient looks odd, but a garbled extraction cannot be assumed. Read the row in Table S-1 before coding it.
2. **DRI PAL band edges (R14 vs R17).** A search of the 2023 report found PAL **examples** of ~1.4 / ~1.6 / ~1.75 / ~2.05 (Table 7-1), with categories built from approximate quartiles of DLW PALs. The report's bands (1.0–<1.4, 1.4–<1.6, 1.6–<1.9, 1.9–<2.5) match the **2005 IOM** definitions. If the 2023 report defines different edges, the activity-level mapping in Option A changes.
3. **O'Neill 2023 athlete meta-analysis (R6).** The abstract names five equations not different from measured RMR: **Cunningham 1980, Harris-Benedict 1918, Cunningham 1991, De Lorenzo**, and one more cut off in the snippet. The report says "among them Cunningham 1980, Cunningham 1991 and ten Haaf". Confirm ten Haaf is the fifth; also note Harris-Benedict 1918 is among them.
4. **Schneditz 2023 (R30).** The paper reports **9,521 days** of measurements. The report says "9,211-day-pair series". Correct the count after checking (the SD figures 0.53% / 0.69% match).
5. **Schoenfeld 2017 link (R97).** The report linked **PubMed 28834797**, which is the Schoenfeld *low- vs high-load* meta-analysis (JSCR 2017), not the weekly-volume meta-analysis. The link was changed to the volume paper's DOI. The volume paper's PMID is believed to be 27433992 (not confirmed).
6. **Pontzer 2016 link (R11).** The report linked a Duke record (…/publications/337837) that search results attach to the **2012 Hadza** paper; the 2016 *Curr Biol* paper appears as …/337797. The link was changed to the 2016 DOI.
7. **Tinsley 2019 journal (R7).** The research notes give *J Sports Sci*; search shows *Appl Physiol Nutr Metab* 44(4):397–406. The report itself names no journal.
8. **Halperin 2022 (R76).** The report says "Lifters underpredict … by 0.95 reps"; the meta-analysis pooled "participants" of mixed training backgrounds. Consider "people" in copy.
9. **Hall 2011 ε = 24 kcal/day per kg (R25).** The 100 kJ/d ≈ 1 kg rule is a steady-state relation between a sustained intake change and eventual weight change (for an average overweight adult). The report uses it as the slope of TEE against weight inside the filter. That is a reasonable approximation but an interpretation; confirm the wording in the paper.
10. **Bajunaid 2025 (R18).** An author correction (https://doi.org/10.1038/s43016-025-01175-2) states the original "contained an error in the application of the equation" to NDNS and NHANES. Check whether the ">50% misreporting" figure survives the correction.
11. **Nunes 2022 protein (R48).** SMD 0.22 is from 62 of the 74 studies. A 2025 authors' reply to a critique cites an "original" overall effect of 0.19 (0.11–0.28). Check which figure applies.
12. **Adaptive-thermogenesis range in the q_T row.** The report attributes "−50 to −230 kcal/day" to Martins 2020 (PMC7657334). Martins reports −92/−38/−7; the −230 upper figure is from **Nunes 2022 (R54)**, whose range is **−65** to −230. The "−50" lower bound has no located source. The citation now points to both; the number was left as is.
13. **Campbell 2020 refeeds (R61).** A published reanalysis (Sainsbury and colleagues; authors' reply https://doi.org/10.3390/jfmk5040086) found no significant group difference in total FFM change, only in dry FFM. The report's "−0.4 vs −1.3 kg FFM" contrast is contested.
14. **Lean-lifter energy-density error (internal arithmetic).** The report says 7700 "overstates energy per kg by about 20%" for 12–15 kg fat. ρ(15) ≈ 6,315 gives 22%, but ρ(12) ≈ 5,900 gives ~30%. "About 20–30%" would be accurate.
15. **Kanellakis 2023 (R33).** Tagged [confirmed], but the 0.45 kg / all-ECW figures were only seen in a non-peer-reviewed blog summary this pass; a 2025 case-control study found no significant cycle-phase water change. Treat as unconfirmed until read.
16. **Ebben 2011 (R93).** Report treats the push-up percentages as a conference paper; the same work appears in *J Strength Cond Res* 25(10):2891–2894. Cite the journal version if its numbers match.
17. **eCFR 101.9 (R72).** The research notes call calories a "Class II nutrient"; calories are in the **third group**, 101.9(g)(5). The report's 20% statement itself is consistent.
18. **WHO WHR cut-offs (R113) and WHO 2020 "≥2 days" (R107).** Both were confirmed only via secondary sources; read the primary tables.
19. **R44 (PMC3856197).** Search could not identify this article, so the [confirmed] tag on the Rivera MPC claim cannot be traced to a title. Open the PMC page.
20. **Westerterp TEF ranges (R20).** The abstract gives DIT of 5–15% of daily EE on a mixed diet; the per-macronutrient ranges (20–30 / 5–10 / 0–3%) were not found in the abstract.
